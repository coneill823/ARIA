# Business plan: {{PROJECT NAME}}

> **In one line:** {{who it is for, what they get, and how it earns — or, said plainly, that this is not a business and what it is instead}}

## 1. The problem, and who has it

{{Whose problem this is — a specific kind of person or organisation, not "everyone" — what it costs them today, and what the evidence is that they have it. If there is no evidence yet, say so: that is the first thing to find out.}}

## 2. The offer

{{What they get, in their words. What it does that the alternatives do not. What it deliberately does not do.}}

## 3. Market and alternatives

{{What people use today, including "nothing" and "a spreadsheet"; the named competitors and what they charge. How big the market is: all the demand there is, the part this can reach, the part it can realistically win — with the arithmetic shown, as a range.}}

## 4. How it earns

{{Who pays, for what, how much and how often — and why that price. If it is not meant to earn (a personal tool, an open-source project), say what it costs to keep going and who bears that.}}

## 5. Reaching customers

{{Where the first ten customers come from, by name or by place. The channels after that, and what each costs in money or time.}}

## 6. What it takes to run

{{What has to be done every week once it exists: support, hosting, supply, upkeep. The tools, suppliers and partners it depends on, and what happens if one of them goes away.}}

## 7. The money

{{What it costs to start, and to run each month. Price minus cost per sale. How many sales cover the fixed costs (fixed costs ÷ margin per sale). A month-by-month view of cash for the first year in a few lines. What money is needed, when, and from where.}}

## 8. Rules and protection

{{The legal form and the registrations it needs; licences; tax; insurance; what personal data it holds and what follows from that; the name, the code and the content — who owns them, under what licence; accessibility. Name the body that decides each where you know it. These are things to check, not advice.}}

## 9. People

{{Who does what. The skills that are missing, and how each gap is filled: learn, hire, partner, or do without.}}

## 10. Risks, and what to test first

{{Imagine it has failed a year from now: the three most likely reasons. For each, the cheapest test that would show it early. The assumptions everything else rests on, most doubtful first.}}

## 11. Milestones and measures

{{The next three to five outcomes, each with the number that shows it was reached. The point at which to stop, and what would be kept if it stops.}}

## 12. Verdict

{{Is this a business, a product, a tool for one person, or not worth doing — and the one thing that would change that answer.}}

## Sources

{{Every claim about the outside world links to where it came from. Anything not sourced is marked as an estimate or an assumption.}}
